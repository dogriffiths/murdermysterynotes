* Day 0: Room I am sitting in (5 mins)

It's a comfortable room. I think most people would agree with that. But it was never going to appear in Homes and Gardens. There was an agreeable amount of chaos. There were blankets over the seats and settee - some for humans and some for cats. Mostly cats. The light was good. Windows on both sides make it pleasant on most days excep the hellish days of Summar. There is a fire - never lit - surrounded by a rotective guard and a series of unlit candles. They used to be lit until Jess arrived. But now they are happily snuffed. A rammel box sits by the TV. Neither is used much but there all the same. So all in all, it is comfortable. And inefficient. And it's home.

* Val MacDermid example
** What sort of novel?
*** Not historical (pacemaker)
*** Crime thriller? He's *going* to commit a murder
*** Who is he? A killer. The instinct "kept him alive"
*** Who is she? Probably a victim, but could be a "surprise" sleuth?
*** Why repeat "as good as"? Alive/dead do not refer to a physical condition, but a mental one. Freedom. The freedom to kill, or the inability to kill. The person sees the ability to murder as freedom and life.
*** Why a nursery rhyme? Common in books. Probably the way he catches people? He is Jack and "falls down the hill", appears to be injured. E.g. Ted Bundy having an arm cast. "Jill comes tumbling after". She will go to his aid and be caught.

* Writing opening paragraph
** A cosy crime
There was a body slumped against the wall. The first snow of February had lightly covered her eyes such that, even if they'd been still able to see, the sight of the spire of St Mary's in Oxenden would have just been a misty shadow to her. The verger muttered a short prayer over her. He knew the vicar, a low churchman, would not approve of praying for the dead, but then he would never know.
** A Brit grit or noir story
There was a body slumped against the wall. This was really inconvenient for Patel in the Oppo post. Was the man dead? Likely not, but he'd still have to call it in, just in case. He just hoped that intelligence made up a decent cover story. Through his zoom lens, he saw the ambulance and police escort arriving 15 minutes later. There was sudden activity in the drugs house. He saw a leather bag tossed out a window to a roadman in the back yard, who quickly pulled back corrugated sheeting and threw it into a pre-dug put. Patel marked it on the map. He knew now where to find her.
** A police procedural
There was a body slumped against a wall. She certainly wasn't going to touch it, and was ordered to make sure no one else did either. While she would ocassionally turn around to check it, her plan was to get through the next hour or so looking at it as little as possible. Was it a suicide? She'd seen her fair share of them, and the wrists had been slit. But where was the blood? Perhaps soaked into the earth of the woodland. Did they have to clear up blood in a wood? Probably not. Particularly in a shitty little council park like this one. Let the rats do it, most likely.
