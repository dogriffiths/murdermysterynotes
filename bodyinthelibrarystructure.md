# The Body in the Library - Structural Outline

## I. Opening Sequence (~5%)
- Opening scene with birthday celebration and implied tragedy
- Establishes post-war setting

## II. Discovery of the Body (~10%)
- Body found in Colonel Bantry's library
- Introduction of Miss Marple
- Initial police investigation
- Identification of victim as Ruby Keene

## III. Investigation Phase One (~25%)
- Introduction of key suspects and locations:
  * The Majestic Hotel
  * Conway Jefferson and his family
  * Josie Turner (Ruby's cousin)
  * Mark Gaskell and Adelaide Jefferson
  * Raymond Starr (dance instructor)
  * George Bartlett
  * Basil Blake
- Establishment of key facts:
  * Ruby was a dance hostess
  * Jefferson planned to adopt Ruby
  * £50,000 inheritance involved

## IV. Second Murder Discovery (~15%)
- Discovery of Pamela Reeves' body in burnt car
- Connection to George Bartlett's missing vehicle
- Investigation of Pamela's "film test" story

## V. Hotel Investigation (~20%)
- Miss Marple's observations at the Majestic
- Interviews with suspects
- Development of relationships between characters
- Revelation of financial motives
- Focus on alibis and timeline

## VI. Basil Blake Confession (~10%)
- Blake admits to moving body
- Reveals he didn't commit murder
- Complication of original theory

## VII. Miss Marple's Solution (~10%)
- Explanation of the double murder plot
- Revelation of Adelaide Jefferson and Josie Turner as killers
- Explanation of body switch and timeline

## VIII. Trap and Resolution (~5%)
- Plan to catch killers using Jefferson as bait
- Capture of murderers
- Final reflections on motives

## Key Themes Throughout:
- Post-war British society
- Class distinctions
- Love and obsession
- Money as motivation
- Appearances vs. reality

## Notable Structural Elements:
1. Dual murder plot
2. Multiple red herrings
3. Parallel investigations (police and Miss Marple)
4. Strategic revelation of information
5. Use of flashbacks in solution sequence

The structure follows a classic British murder mystery format, with the initial crime complicated by a second murder midway through, leading to a complex solution that ties both deaths together. The pacing allocates significant time to character development and the establishment of relationships, which proves crucial to understanding the final solution.
