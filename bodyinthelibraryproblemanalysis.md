# Major Problems and Their Solutions

## 1. The Mystery of the Body in Bantry's Library

### Initial Problem:
- Unidentified young woman's body found in Colonel Bantry's library
- Damage to Bantry's reputation
- No apparent connection to location

### Key Decisions Leading to Solution:
1. Miss Marple's decision to:
   - Question why the body was placed there
   - Investigate Basil Blake connection
2. Basil Blake's decision to confess about moving the body
3. Police decision to broaden investigation to the Majestic Hotel

### Resolution:
- Body was meant to be planted at Basil Blake's house
- Blake's impulsive "prank" disrupted original murder plan
- Bantry's reputation salvaged through explanation

## 2. The Ruby Keene Murder

### Initial Problem:
- Identifying the killer(s)
- Establishing timeline
- Understanding motive

### Key Decisions Leading to Solution:
1. Miss Marple's crucial decisions:
   - Focus on fingernail evidence
   - Question identification of body
   - Investigate Josie Turner's anger
2. Police decision to investigate Jefferson family finances
3. Conway Jefferson's decision to reveal adoption plans
4. Florence's decision to share information about Pamela's "screen test"

### Resolution:
- Adelaide Jefferson and Josie Turner identified as killers
- Motive revealed as inheritance money and romantic relationship
- Complete timeline established through witness accounts

## Lessons from Problem-Solving Patterns:

1. Initial Appearances vs. Reality
- Most problems initially appeared simpler than they were
- Solutions required looking past surface explanations
- Critical thinking and questioning assumptions proved vital

2. Information Flow
- Key breaks came from unexpected sources
- Sharing of information crucial to solutions
- Personal connections revealed important details

3. Decision Chain Effects
- Small decisions had large consequences
- Multiple decisions combined to create solutions
- Timing of decisions proved crucial

4. Problem Interconnection
- Most problems connected to central murder plot
- Solutions to smaller problems led to larger answers
- Understanding relationships between problems crucial to overall solution

This analysis shows how the resolution of each problem depended on both individual decisions and the interconnection between various choices made by different characters. The success of the investigation relied heavily on Miss Marple's ability to connect seemingly unrelated problems into a coherent whole.
